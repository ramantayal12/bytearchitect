---
title: Requirements and Estimation
---

A URL shortener turns a long address such as `https://example.com/articles/2024/how-we-scaled-our-database?utm_source=newsletter` into a short one like `https://sho.rt/aZ3kP9x`. When someone visits the short link, the service redirects them to the original. It is one of the most common warm-up design problems because it is easy to understand yet raises real questions about key generation, storage, caching and abuse.

## Functional requirements

- **Shorten:** given a long URL, return a unique short URL.
- **Redirect:** visiting a short URL redirects to the original long URL.
- **Custom aliases:** users may choose their own short key, such as `sho.rt/launch`, if it is available.
- **Expiration:** links may have an expiry time, after which they stop redirecting.
- **Deletion:** the creator can delete a link.
- **Analytics (optional):** the creator can see click counts over time, by country and referrer.

## Non-functional requirements

- **High availability for redirects.** If redirects fail, every link ever shared breaks. Redirects matter far more than creation.
- **Low latency for redirects**, ideally a few milliseconds of server time.
- **Unpredictable keys.** Short keys should not be guessable in sequence, so people cannot enumerate private links.
- **Durability.** Once created, a mapping must not be lost.
- **Scalability** to billions of links.

## Capacity estimation

| Assumption | Value |
| --- | --- |
| New short URLs per month | 200 million |
| Read-to-write ratio | 100:1 |
| Retention | 5 years by default |
| Average record size | 500 bytes (long URL, key, owner, timestamps) |

**Writes:** 200 million ÷ (30 × 86,400) ≈ 77 new URLs per second on average.

**Reads:** 100 × 77 ≈ 7,700 redirects per second on average; plan for peaks of perhaps 20,000 to 30,000 per second when links go viral.

**Total links over 5 years:** 200 million × 12 × 5 = 12 billion.

**Storage:** 12 billion × 500 bytes = 6 TB, plus indexes and replication. This fits comfortably in a sharded key-value store.

**Bandwidth:** redirect responses are tiny, well under 1 KB, so 30,000 per second is only about 15 to 30 MB per second.

**Cache:** following the 80/20 rule, caching the hottest 20 percent of daily redirect keys is plenty. With about 660 million redirects per day, of which a much smaller number of distinct URLs, caching even 100 million entries at 500 bytes is 50 GB, which fits on a few cache nodes.

## How long should keys be?

Keys use the 62 characters `[a-z A-Z 0-9]`. The number of possible keys grows quickly with length:

| Key length | Possible keys |
| --- | --- |
| 6 | 62⁶ ≈ 56.8 billion |
| 7 | 62⁷ ≈ 3.5 trillion |
| 8 | 62⁸ ≈ 218 trillion |

Six characters would cover 12 billion links, but the key space would be over 20 percent used, making random collisions frequent and enumeration of existing links easy. **Seven characters** give a key space about 300 times larger than needed, keeping collisions rare and keys hard to guess.

<Callout type="interview">
Always derive the key length from your estimates. Showing that 62⁷ comfortably exceeds the number of links you expect demonstrates that your design choices follow from numbers, not habit.
</Callout>

## What a record looks like

| Field | Type | Size | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| key | string (7 chars) | 7 B | Partition key |
| long_url | string | ~100–2,000 B | Average ~200 B |
| owner_id | int64 | 8 B | Nullable for anonymous links |
| created_at, expires_at | timestamps | 16 B | `expires_at` drives lazy expiry |
| flags | small int | 1–4 B | Custom alias, disabled, flagged as unsafe |

With indexes, replication metadata and storage-engine overhead, ~500 bytes per record is a comfortable planning figure.

<Ponder question="The estimate says reads are 100 times writes. Which component does that ratio most influence?">
The cache. With 100 reads per write and a strongly skewed popularity distribution, most redirects can be answered from memory, so the key-value store mainly serves cache misses and new writes. It also justifies optimizing the redirect path relentlessly (in-process caches, regional deployment) while keeping the creation path simple.
</Ponder>

<KeyTakeaways>
- A URL shortener maps short keys to long URLs and redirects visitors.
- Redirect availability and latency matter far more than link creation.
- Estimates: about 77 writes and 7,700 redirects per second on average, and about 12 billion links (6 TB) over five years.
- Redirect traffic is small in bytes and highly cacheable.
- Seven base-62 characters give about 3.5 trillion keys, ample headroom for 12 billion links.
</KeyTakeaways>
